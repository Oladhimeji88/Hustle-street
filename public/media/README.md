# Landing media

Everything in this folder except the `app-*.png` screenshots is a **branded
placeholder**, generated by `pnpm media` from `scripts/generate-media.ts`.

They exist so the landing page renders complete and on-brand before a
photographer or editor has been near it. They are not a substitute for real
media, and shipping them to a live marketplace would undersell the product.

---

## Replace in this order

### 1. `story-*.png` — hustler stories

The single highest-value assets on the page. A real person saying what changed
for them does more for trust than every feature paragraph combined.

- Portrait, 3:4, minimum 1080×1440
- Shoot on location — a plumber in a kitchen, a designer at their desk
- **Get written consent** for name, face, trade and area before publishing

> The quotes in `src/lib/config/media.ts` are illustrative placeholders. Replace
> them with real, consented testimonials. Do not ship an invented quote
> attributed to a named person.

### 2. `poster-*.png` — video poster frames

Pulled from the actual footage once it exists. 16:9, 1920×1080.

### 3. `scene-*.png` — section photography

Real Lagos. Someone moving a sofa down a staircase, a makeup artist working, a
dispatch rider in traffic. 16:10, minimum 1600×1000.

### 4. `app-*.png` — product screenshots

These are faithful SVG renderings of the real UI, so they stay useful. When the
UI moves meaningfully, either update the drawing in `scripts/generate-media.ts`
or replace them with genuine device captures at 390×844 @2×.

### 5. `coverage-lagos.png` — coverage map

Fine as an abstract for now. Replace with a real styled map when the tile
provider is chosen.

## Adding video

There is no video on the page yet. `VideoPlayer` renders the poster with an
honest "Film coming soon" badge whenever `sources` is empty, so nothing looks
broken — but nobody is watching anything either.

To ship a real film:

1. **Encode two versions.** WebM (VP9) first, MP4 (H.264) as the Safari
   fallback. Target roughly 1.5 Mbps at 1080p.

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -i master.mov -c:v libvpx-vp9 -b:v 1500k -c:a libopus how-it-works.webm
   ffmpeg -i master.mov -c:v libx264  -b:v 1500k -c:a aac   how-it-works.mp4
   ```

2. **Export captions** as WebVTT into `captions/`.

3. **Register them** in `src/lib/config/media.ts`:

   ```ts
   sources: [
     { src: '/media/how-it-works.webm', type: 'video/webm' },
     { src: '/media/how-it-works.mp4',  type: 'video/mp4'  },
   ],
   captionsSrc: '/media/captions/how-it-works.vtt',
   ```

### Two rules the player enforces

**Nothing autoplays, ever.** `preload="none"` means not a single byte of video
is fetched until someone taps play. Most of our users are on metered mobile
data — autoplaying a hero video spends a stranger's airtime to show them
marketing.

**Captions are not optional.** Most people watch muted, and a video without
captions is unusable for anyone deaf or hard of hearing. Every entry also
carries a `transcript`, which is what actually communicates when the video
fails to load at all.

## Regenerating

```bash
pnpm media    # this folder
pnpm icons    # PWA icons + og.png
```

Both are deterministic, so re-running produces identical output.
